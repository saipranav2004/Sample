Architecture

What happens during one tool call.

The agent asks to use a tool. Before the tool sees the request, Zenstra confirms the agent's identity, screens the content it is acting on, evaluates policy and intent, and writes the decision to the ledger. This example is the email attempt from the homepage.

Deployment

Three ways in. Most institutions use two.

Agents you build take the SDK. Agents you buy or connect over MCP go through the gateway. Copilots on employee devices are covered by the endpoint app. All three report to the same policy set and ledger.

Securing Zenstra

A control in the path of every action has to be trustworthy itself.

The first question a CISO asks about an inline control is what happens when it is wrong, slow or compromised. These are the answers.

Your data stays with you

Prompts and tool payloads are evaluated inside your environment. The control plane receives decisions and redacted evidence only.

Fail-closed, with an exit

If a decision point is unreachable, actions are held. Low-risk tools can be set to fail-open, and those decisions are recorded on recovery.

No single key opens everything

Per-tenant encryption keys, per-agent identities, and console access through your identity provider with role-based permissions.

A ledger you can verify

Each entry is chained to the previous one. You can verify the chain independently with the open-source verifier.

Tested from outside

Annual independent penetration test and a public vulnerability disclosure policy with a security.txt.

Software you can inspect

A software bill of materials ships with every SDK, gateway and endpoint release.
